# Problem Solving

**Q1: Do you prefer to solve problems on your own or ask for help?**

- Used to prefer asking people for help; now relies more on AI
- Quote: _"with AI I ask AI for help more, which is like easy. Accessible."_

**Q2: How long after starting a problem do you turn to AI?**

- Tries to solve it without AI at least once
- After 10 to 15 minutes stuck, opens the AI
- Quote: _"I try to do it without AI at least once"_

**Q3: Can you show an example of a recent problem?**

- Example: stochastic models course, an exam that required genuine insight rather than mechanical solving
- Strategy: rereads all of the professor's notes to find relevant properties and formulas
- Quote: _"there's probably some property, some formula in there that will be useful to me"_
- Note: the example was from an exam, not an assignment; the interviewer tried to redirect to an assignment, but the participant went back to the exam context

**Q4: How often did you feel stuck on the content while studying the notes?**

- Partly off-target answer: said they felt stuck _before_ looking at the notes, and that the notes unblocked them
- Inference (not directly stated): the notes worked as the first unblocking resource, before AI

**Q5: What is your step-by-step process for solving a problem?**

- Identifies what the problem is asking
- Checks whether they have solved something similar before; if so, applies the same method
- If they have never seen the problem, tries to identify which part of the course material it draws on
- Quote: _"I try to figure out what the problem is asking and see if I've done it some way before"_

**Q6: What was good about your method of using AI?**

- Speed and accessibility: no need to physically go anywhere
- Available 24 hours
- No embarrassment about asking basic questions
- Quotes: _"you can ask it the dumbest question and it will answer you"_; _"you won't be embarrassed to show that you didn't pay attention in any class"_

**Q7: What was AI not good enough at teaching you?**

- When it didn't have the course material as context, the AI brought in irrelevant outside content
- Quote: _"it brought in topics that weren't from the class to answer a problem"_
- Example: used formulas that didn't belong to the course's universe and wouldn't be useful on the exam
- Note: the interviewer suggested the probability formula example; the participant confirmed it but did not come up with it spontaneously

**Q8: What worked best about the "walk me through like a TA" setup?**

- Could actually understand the content without being given the direct answer
- Kept control of the process: they guided the AI, not the other way around
- Quotes: _"I was still trying to solve the problem, even with help"_; _"yes"_ (when asked if they felt they were guiding the AI)
- Method: gave the AI all of the professor's notes as context; instructed it to point to which lecture or content to look up, without giving the solution

**Q9: Was there a frustrating moment using AI?**

- Direct answer: _"to be honest, not in 2026"_

**Q10: How often do you ask AI for the final answer?**

- About 25% of the time (_"a quarter"_)
- Trigger: not a fixed amount of time, but the feeling of being completely stuck
- Quote: _"it's not really about time, I think it's more about being stuck"_
- Acknowledged consequence: less retention in these cases; knows they will need to review before the exam

# Afterthought

**Q1: How do you feel after studying 4 hours with AI?**

- Doesn't feel guilty; the use is active, not passive
- Quote: _"I ask it to quiz me, so it's not passive"_
- Ways of using it: AI generates exercises, corrects answers, identifies gaps

**Q2: What helps and what hinders you most when studying?**

- Hinders: any distraction, lack of focus
- Helps: an environment that supports focus, the urgency of having to study
- AI as a distraction: doesn't blame AI for procrastination, but admits the time between prompts _may_ increase the tendency to get distracted
- Quote: _"if I were going to procrastinate, it's not because of this that I'm procrastinating"_

**Q3: Do you feel you should study longer without AI?**

- Yes, recognizes they should put in more independent effort
- Quote: _"I feel like sometimes maybe I give up a bit before I really need to"_
- Doesn't feel guilty about current use
- Not directly covered: learning outside of school and independence on similar problem types without AI

# Notes on How the Interview Went

**What worked well**

- The participant was communicative and reflective, especially about the distinct ways of using AI (exercises vs. exam prep)
- The stochastic models example produced rich, spontaneous quotes

**Problems identified**

- Frequent leading prompts: the interviewer suggested answers before the participant finished formulating them
  - Examples: suggested "minimization, statistics, probability" in Profiling Q2; suggested the probability formula example in Problem Solving Q7; suggested "40 minutes" as a threshold in Q10
  - Risk: answers the participant confirmed may reflect the interviewer's suggestion rather than their actual experience
- Excessive rephrasing: several questions were repeated in different ways before being understood (especially Profiling Q4 and Problem Solving Q10), which may have influenced the final answer
- Loss of focus: the questions about learning outside of school and independence on similar problems were not asked directly and remain **uncovered**
- Implicit shift in the script: the script seemed originally aimed at help from people (peers, professors), but the participant moved the focus to AI early on; the interviewer followed that shift without systematically returning to the human-help dimension

**Suggestions for next interviews**

- Wait for the participant to finish answering before offering examples or categories
- Explicitly include questions about learning outside the school context
- Ask directly: "Can you solve problems of the same type independently after using AI?" to capture retention more precisely
- Clearly separate questions about human help and AI help in the script
