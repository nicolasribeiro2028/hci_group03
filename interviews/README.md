# Interviews

Interview reports for the project. After each interview, copy [_template.md](_template.md) to `YYYY-MM-DD_participant-code.md` (e.g. `2026-10-05_P01.md`) and fill it in within an hour, while it's fresh. Use a participant code or initials, not full names.

## Goal

Understand what makes help useful or unhelpful for STEM students when they are trying to learn or solve problems.

## Objectives

- Understand how people deal with asking for help.
- Understand what people's study methods are:
  - solving problems
  - studying for exams
  - learning in general (outside of school)
- Understand when help feels insufficient or sufficient, and what criteria people use to judge it.

> **Interviewer note (do not read aloud):** We are trying to understand how people get help in general, not specifically from AI, so we can apply some of it to prevent people from using AI in ways that make them learn less.

## Interview outline (15 to 20 min)

### Profiling

1. What are you studying, and what kinds of problems or subjects do you usually work on?
2. How difficult have your courses been so far? (1 to 5, where 1 is very easy and 5 is very hard)
3. Do you usually ask for help quickly, or do you try to solve problems completely on your own?
4. (If they try on their own first) How long do you usually try before you look for help?

### Problem solving

1. Walk me through the last time you got really stuck on a STEM problem. What happened from the moment you got stuck until you moved on?
2. When you get stuck while learning or solving a problem, what do you usually do first?
3. (If help wasn't mentioned in Q2) Can you tell me about a recent time when you were stuck and received help? (Probe: who or what did the help come from, such as a friend, TA, professor, video, forum, textbook, or AI tool?)
4. (If yes on Q3) What made that help useful to you?
5. (If the help wasn't good) What made it not useful?
6. After receiving that help, did you feel you understood the problem better, or did it mainly help you complete the task? Why?
7. (Skip if already covered in Q5) Can you tell me about a time when help was not useful, or made things more confusing or frustrating?
8. Thinking about that unhelpful (or only partly helpful) help, what would you have wanted the person or resource to do differently?
9. How do you decide when to keep trying on your own and when to ask for help?
10. After receiving help on a problem, did you need help again with a similar problem, or could you solve it on your own the next time?

### Afterthought

1. What kind of help makes you feel like you are still solving the problem yourself rather than having someone else solve it for you?
2. Thinking across your experiences, what do you think makes the biggest difference between helpful and unhelpful support when learning?
3. Have you ever looked back and thought "I should have struggled with that longer" or "I wasted too much time before asking"? Tell me about it.

## Open decisions for the team

- **Timing:** 17 questions for 15 to 20 minutes is a lot. Mark some as "must ask" and others as "if time". Problem solving Q6 and Q10 and Afterthought Q1 and Q2 look most useful for our goal.
- **Intro script:** add a short script explaining the class and the purpose of the interview, plus a consent line if recording.
- **Possible leading question:** Afterthought Q3 suggests an answer. If people just agree, ask it neutrally, e.g. "Looking back, would you have done anything differently?"
- **Overlap:** Problem solving Q9 overlaps with Profiling Q3 and Q4; consider keeping only one version.

## Tips

- Keep template questions 6 (quotes) and 10 (how the interview went) short but specific. Quotes feed the write-up; question 10 helps refine the outline between interviews.
