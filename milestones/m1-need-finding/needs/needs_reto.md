## key needs [4-6 needs]:

-   AI explanations are often too long/in-depth <--AH-HA NEED.

    quote by reto_1 (translated from german): "...it's helpful when the help arrives step by step and not all at once...".
    And reto_2 mentioned that an AI often doesn't fully understand the intent of the question directly and should first give a short summary answer and then further prompts could go deeper.
    noah_1 also mentioned that answers that are too long or complex are unhelpful.

    This is important because an AI giving away too much of the answer will prevent the student from solving the exercise by themself.
    And it is important because a long answer that goes in a slightly different direction than expected can confuse the student rather than help them.


-   There's a need for knowing when you should not fully trust an AI's answers.

    Some of the interviewees, such as franco_1, mentioned completely trusting in the answers provided by AIs.
    quote by franco_2 which emphasizes this trust in AI: "My peers make mistakes, unlike AI, hence I go directly to it".

    This is important because an AI often gives incorrect answers with just as much confidence as if they were correct.


-   Tools are often unaware of what prior knowledge the student already has.

    noah_1 said that AI answers are unhelpful when they assume the user already knows a topic that they actually don't know.
    noah_2 also mentioned that help becomes less useful when it assumes too much prior knowledge.

    This is important because AIs will generate answers that can be effectively useless to the user and it wastes time becasue now the user needs to specify what they know and prompt again.


## secondary needs:

-   AI solutions are either too vague or they directly provide the answer.

    (source franco)


-   There's a need for some more pushback by the tool before giving answers.

    Some of the students (such as franco_1) mentioned that they only spend a very short time trying to solve the problem by themself (< 1min) and quickly use AI for help.
    quote from nicolas_1: "I feel like sometimes maybe I give up a bit before I really need to".


-   AI generated code can be hard to understand when you don't know the implementation details or concepts.

    franco_1 said that he didn't know how to correct the code that an AI gave him.


-   AIs should point you in the right direciton and tell you where to look rather than give you the solution.

    (source valentin_1)


-   There's a need for an AI that is not as solution oriented.
    
    valentin_2 mentioned that AI's are too focused on giving you the solution to a problem.

    This is important because when studying the student should be the one coming up with the solution.


-   AI tools are often unaware of the lecture context.

    nicolas_1 said the AI used formulas that didn't really belong to the coures and would've been unhelpful during the exam.
