# Interview report — P01

**Date:** 2026-10-06  
**Goal:** Understand what makes help useful or unhelpful for STEM students learning or solving problems.

## Profiling

1. **Studies and problems:** The participant described studying computational biology, including computational simulations and molecular interactions. They also mentioned coursework in calculus, linear algebra, chemistry, and dynamics.
2. **Course difficulty:** Rated it around 4–5 out of 5.
3. **When they ask for help:** Tries a problem on their own first, typically for about an hour.

## Problem solving

1. **Recent time stuck:** Struggled to understand a value involving the image of a function. Asked Claude for an explanation and an example, then was able to solve the problem.
2. **What they do first:** Focuses on learning how the concept works before trying to finish the problem. If an example is not enough, may ask for a direct solution.
3. **What made the help useful:** Prompts Claude to act as a tutor and provide explanations and additional context. Said this approach helps them understand the content, not merely complete the task.
4. **When help falls short:** Claude sometimes stops following earlier instructions in a long chat. For writing, finds it frustrating when an AI changes their word choices while supposedly correcting grammar, because it does not preserve their voice. Also mentioned frustration with image generation.
5. **Transfer to similar problems:** Can handle very similar problems, but changes in wording or framing can make applying what they learned difficult.
6. **How they decide to seek help:** Stated threshold is roughly one hour of trying on their own.

## Afterthought

- **Helpful versus unhelpful support:** Prefers being walked through the line of thinking or strategy rather than receiving only the answer.
- **Looking back:** Did not say they wished they had struggled longer; felt an hour of trying before asking was enough.

## Short quotes (translated from Portuguese)

> "I focus on learning first, then I solve it."

> "I usually write a prompt asking it to act as a tutor."

> "Sometimes I ask it to correct the grammar and it goes around changing the words."

## Gaps and interview notes

- The interview captured a concrete example of AI help, but not a comparable example involving a friend, TA, professor, textbook, or other non-AI resource.
- Exam study methods and learning outside school were not captured.
- A useful follow-up would be to ask them to work through a *differently worded* problem after receiving help, to clarify where transfer breaks down.
